EN12-01 - Managing Emergency Water Storage on a Campus reserve, but it did not specify how often containers should be inspected or who was responsible for replacing them. A working group therefore mapped the locations of tanks, portable containers and drinking-water dispensers across eight buildings. The review focused on short interruptions lasting less than one day, because longer emergencies would require outside supply and a different distribution plan Storage and Inspection Rules The group estimated a minimum planning quantity of 3 litres of drinking water per person for a 24- hour period, with additional water needed for basic hygiene and food preparation Rather than filling every available container, staff selected food-grade vessels that could be sealed, labelled and moved safely Each label showed the fill date, intended use and responsible building. Containers were kept away from direct sunlight, cleaning chemicals and fuel. The largest portable vessel weighed almost 24 kg when full, so it was placed on a low trolley instead of a high shelf Inspection became the most important part of the new plan On the first Monday of every month, a building representative checked seals, leaks, labels and storage conditions Any container with a damaged cap, unusual odour or unclear history was emptied and cleaned rather than kept for convenience. The fixed tanks were included in the facilities maintenance schedule, with records of cleaning dates and inlet-screen condition A small sample of stored water was also checked after long periods of non-use according to the campus health office procedure. A short exercise was held on 14 August 2026. One residence floor was told that tap water was unavailable for two hours, and volunteers practised retrieving, carrying and issuing the stored supply. The exercise revealed that the written map was accurate but one trolley could not pass through a narrow service doorway Moving two containers to a different room solved the problem The group also learned that people needed clear instructions about which reserve was suitable for drinking and which was intended only for cleaning. The final plan treated stored water as a managed resource rather than a pile of containers. It assigned ownership, inspection intervals. safe lifting arrangements and a method for recording use The reserve was modest, but the process reduced confusion during routine interruptions and provided a stronger starting point for larger emergency planning.
