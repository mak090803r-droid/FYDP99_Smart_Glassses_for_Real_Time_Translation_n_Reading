EN12-02 - A Pilot Bicycle Counter on a Campus Road A transport research class installed a temporary bicycle counter on a campus road to compare manual observations with an electronic sensing system. The site was chosen because it carred steady bicycle traffic but had only one vehicle lane in each direction, making visual verification practical Two students marked a 12 m observation zone with removable tape and positioned the sensor so that passing bicycles crossed its detection area at a consistent angle. Data collection began at 07 30 on 3 September 2026 and continued until 18:30. For validation, observers counted bicycles manually during six separate thirty-minute windows They recorded direction, bicycle type and whether two riders passed side by side The electronic counter stored a timestamp and direction estimate for every detected event. During the moming period. the system counted 146 bicycles while the observers counted 150 Review of the uimestamps showed that most missed events occurred when pairs travelled close together A second issue appeared when a grounds vehicle moved slowly along the edge of the detection zone and produced two false bicycle events. Installation Adjustment The class adjusted the installation rather than changing the data after collection. The sensor was rotated by 7 degrees and moved 0.6 m farther from the road edge so that maintenance vehicles were less likely to enter its strongest detection region. The next validation window produced 132 electronic counts against 134 manual counts. Side-by-side riders still presented a challenge. but the false vehicle detections stopped. Students noted that a small numerical improvement was useful only because the physical reason for the change was understood and documented Daily traffic patterns were also informative. The largest thirty-minute total occurred between 08 10 and 08 40, while a second peak appeared after classes ended. Rain beginning at 15:20 reduced bicycle traffic by approximately 38% compared with the previous dry hour. The class did not treat this single day as representative of an entire semester: instead, it used the exercise to practise installation, validation and uncertainty reporting. At the end of the pilot, all temporary markings were removed and the dataset was archived with a site photograph, sensor orientation sketch and calibration notes. The main lesson was that automated counters can reduce labour, but they sill require field checks. Without manual observations, the class would not have known whether an unusual count reflected real travel behaviour, a passing service vehicle or limitations in separating closely spaced riders
